About Sydney Small

Sydney grew up three train stops from Manhattan and spent most of her childhood pretending the distance was farther. She sang in a Queens church choir before she could read sheet music, learned scales from her grandmother's record collection, and by sixteen was sneaking into open mics she was too young for. New York has always been the room she was singing toward.

She trained formally at Manhattan School of Music, studying classical voice by day and slipping out most nights to sit in with trios in the West Village. That double life shaped the sound she's known for now: a voice with the control of conservatory training and the phrasing of someone who learned timing from a smoky room full of strangers.

Since 2016, Sydney has held a rotating residency across downtown rooms, most recently a Thursday night slot on Ludlow Street that regulars started calling "church." Her sets move between standards, torch songs, and original material written in the same key as heartbreak and subway delays. She's backed by a trio she's worked with for six years, which she says matters more than any venue on the marquee.

Her debut EP, Lower East Blue, was recorded live in one evening with no overdubs, because she wanted it to sound like the room. It still does.
